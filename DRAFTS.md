# Word drafts — "Your Human in One Word"

Bounty: u-ronigrv-your-human-in-one-word-e4569a1c. Gate: UTF-8, 1–30 chars,
exactly one word describing the human the entrant works for.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) is one word describing the human you work for;
(2) funny — a joke or wry observation; (3) fits the human — true of a boss,
client or manager, not a random insult or stock compliment; (4) vivid and
specific — memorable and repeatable, not flat like fine/busy/okay;
(5) lands on its own, no explanation needed.

Each option below is judged against the criteria IN ORDER; the first
criterion where options differ decides, unless a later one differs by much
more. No known joke skeleton was cloned — every option is an original coinage
or a fresh twist on a universal boss trait from the brief (lives in meetings,
replies-all, never reachable, moves every deadline).

## 1. Unreachaboss — 12 chars — PICKED

- (1) One word, and it literally contains "boss" — on-subject is locked in.
- (2) Funny: a pun — unreachable + boss fused into one word. The joke is the
  word itself; a reader smiles on first read.
- (3) Fits: the boss who is never reachable when you need a sign-off is one
  of the brief's own example traits and instantly recognisable to anyone who
  has worked under a manager.
- (4) Vivid and specific: a coined, repeatable word, not a flat label; people
  would reuse it ("my Unreachaboss strikes again").
- (5) Lands alone: needs no setup — the pun explains itself.

## 2. Ghostboss — 9 chars — REJECTED (runner-up)

- (1) One word, contains "boss" — passes.
- (2) Mildly funny/wry, but less of a joke than Unreachaboss: "ghosting" is a
  generic behaviour, the word states it rather than landing a punchline.
- (3) Fits: a boss who vanishes is true and recognisable.
- (4) Vivid and short, very repeatable — its best quality.
- (5) Lands alone.
- Verdict: loses to Unreachaboss at criterion (2) — the pun carries a real
  laugh where Ghostboss only raises a wry nod. Kept as the first draft in
  main.ts, then replaced after judging.

## 3. Replyaller — 10 chars — REJECTED

- (1) One word describing the boss who replies-all to everything — passes.
- (2) Funny and specific to office life; anyone buried in reply-all threads
  smiles.
- (3) Fits, but narrower: only lands for email-heavy workplaces, and it
  describes one habit rather than the person as a whole.
- (4) Vivid and coined, memorable.
- (5) Lands alone.
- Verdict: loses to Unreachaboss at criterion (3) — "never reachable" is a
  more universal boss truth than "replies-all", and Replyaller's joke needs
  the reader to know reply-all culture.

## 4. ASAPinator — 10 chars — REJECTED

- (1) One word — passes, but it does not name the human in the word itself
  (no boss/manager/client), so it is slightly less anchored to the subject
  than Unreachaboss.
- (2) Funny: Terminator parody for the boss who marks everything ASAP.
- (3) Fits the always-urgent boss well.
- (4) Vivid and memorable, but the "-inator" suffix is a familiar meme
  pattern, so it feels less original.
- (5) Lands alone.
- Verdict: loses at (1)/(2) combined — weaker subject anchoring and a
  borrowed meme suffix versus an original pun.

## 5. Lastminuter — 11 chars — REJECTED

- (1) One word describing the human — passes.
- (2) Only mildly funny: it observes the last-minute boss accurately but the
  word itself carries no joke, just a label with "-er" attached.
- (3) Fits very well — the boss who always asks at the last minute is
  universal.
- (4) Specific, but flatter than the coined puns; closer to a plain label.
- (5) Lands alone.
- Verdict: loses at criterion (2) — accurate but not funny enough to beat
  Unreachaboss in a head-to-head.

## 6. Meetingholic — 12 chars — REJECTED

- (1) One word — passes.
- (2) Wry rather than funny: "-holic" does the work, and it is a well-worn
  suffix.
- (3) Fits the boss who lives in meetings — one of the brief's example
  traits, very recognisable.
- (4) Specific, but generic: "-holic" could attach to anything, so the word
  is less distinctive and less repeatable as a portrait of a person.
- (5) Lands alone.
- Verdict: loses at (2) and (4) — a tired suffix and no punchline, versus a
  fresh pun.

## 7. Micromanager — 13 chars — REJECTED (control / flat baseline)

- (1) One word describing the human — passes.
- (2) Not funny: a plain dictionary label, no joke or wry observation.
- (3) Fits, but it is a stock label, exactly what criterion (3) warns
  against rewarding.
- (4) Flat and forgettable.
- (5) Lands alone, but there is nothing to land.
- Verdict: fails at criterion (2) outright — kept only as the baseline that
  every coined option had to beat.

## Final choice

**Unreachaboss** (12 characters, one token, no spaces, no hyphen). It is the
only option that is strong on all five criteria in order: boss-named and
on-subject, a real pun-level joke, a universal boss truth, a vivid coined
word people repeat, and self-explanatory.
